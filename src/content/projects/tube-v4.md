<h3 class="text-4xl font-bold text-white">Component Testing</h3>
<p class="text-zinc-400 text-lg leading-relaxed">
One thing I haven't said which is kind of important is that with this tradition for some reason I added a rule for myself which did make things more difficult. I never wanted to test anything before the day it floated. I found it exciting to find out if the tube was a success or not once it hit the water for the first time. <br />
This year I only slightly broke that rule and tested the motors in my bathtub. I custom 3D designed and printed pillow blocks, propellers, and drive shafts (Shout out Colorado Springs Library!). To find what propeller worked the best (if any at all) I made a rig that I could hold in my bathtub to see how much force each propeller made.
</p>

<h3 class="text-4xl font-bold text-white">On the water!</h3>
<p class="text-zinc-400 text-lg leading-relaxed">
Sadly I don't seem to have any photos or videos of the tube on the water this year... So I had my neice make me an extremely high quality render of what it looked like.

The motors worked just enough to actually push the tube which was perfect. I was able to buzz around slowly for around 15 minutes before my controllers began to overheat and I figured it was time to head in. Luckily I expected this and was able to easily take off many parts and comfortably float just like the previous year.
